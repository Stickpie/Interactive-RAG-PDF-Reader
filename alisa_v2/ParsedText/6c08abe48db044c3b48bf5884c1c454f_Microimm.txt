Topic 7-2

    ●​  Effective helper (Th) T cells help other cells by “telling” them how to eliminate pathogens
        via release of cytokines
           ○​ Dn destroy pathogens directly

    ●​ Many diff types of Effector Th cells
           ○​  For a naive T cell to know which type of helper cell to become - all dictated by the
             mature dendritic cell (DC)
           ○​ Mature DC 1) bind to naive T helper cells and 2) secrete polarizing cytokines
                  telling that naive T helper cell to become (in this case) an effector T helper 1 cell
               (yellow one)





    ●​ Role of Effector T Helper 1 (Th1) cells is to eliminate intracellular bacteria that are living
        in the phagosome/phagolysosome of macrophages
           ○​  (Recall: those intracellular bacteria that are in the cytoplasm of the phagocyte will
            be destroyed by the effector cytotoxic T cells)
                   ■​  Effector Th1 cells are ineffective here

    ●​ Why are macrophages predominantly the cell infected by intracellular bacteria?
           ○​ Bc many intracellular bacteria require iron for growth/survival in host cells
           ○​ Macrophages are important in normal iron homeostasis - they phagocytose RBC
              (dn need to know for exam)

    ●​ Some intracellular bacteria are still destroyed by lysosomal contents - so there is always
      gonna be some exogenous bacterial peptide being presented on MHC class 2 molecules
      by the infected macrophage

--- Page 2 ---
       ○​  Effector Th1 cells bind to peptide on the MHC class 2 molec, and secrete
           cytokine: IFN-γ (gamma)
       ○​  IFN-y will bind to interferon gamma receptor present on the surface of the
         macrophage
               ■​  this sends activation signal to the infected macrophage to tell it to
                 increase lysosome-phagosome fusion -> more acids and lysosomal
              enzyme released onto that intracellular bacteria
       ○​  IFN-y also tells macrophage to increase production of toxic enzymes (NADPH
          oxidase and nitric oxide synthetase) to incre production of toxic oxygen
           metabolites and nitric oxide





●​ However, that is not v. destructive for 100% of the intracellular bacteria

●​  Another role of Effector Th1 cell is to “wall off”/ surround the infected macrophages in the
    tissue and “suffocate” the bacteria
       ○​  Called granuloma
       ○​  Blocks entry of nutrients and O2 -> starves the intracellular bacteria
       ○​  Kills infected macrophages but dn always kill the bacteria (ie tuberculosis -> TB
          remains latent for life - we cannot 100% kill these pathogens)

Topic 7-3

    ●​ Immature dendritic cells reside in blood & tissues
           ○​  Includes peripheral tissue and secondary lymphoid tissue
    ●​ Pathogen can enter a dendritic cell by:
           ○​  1) Phagocytosis (extracellular bacteria)- peptides presented on MHC class 2
            molecs
           ○​  2) Viral entry or intracellular bacteria (DC infected) - peptides presented on MHC
              class 1 molecs

    ●​ Immature DC “internalize” pathogens in peripheral tissues
    ●​ Some DC exit tissues and migrate via lymphatics to lymph node
    ●​ DCs mature during migration & become APC (Antigen-Presenting Cell) (aka mature DC)
    ●​ Mature DC enters T cell area of Lymph Node (LN) and present its peptides on MHC
       molecules to naive T cells





    ●​ Some immature DC are resident in SLT (secondary lymphoid tissue) & meet pathogens
       entering the spleen (via blood), lymph node (via lymph = pathogens in peripheral tissues),
      and MALT (via mucosa)

--- Page 4 ---
●​ Naive T cell will interact w/ a migrant peripheral DC at the lymph node, or they will
    interact w/ a resident DC at the spleen, lymph node, or the MALT tissue

●​ 1 naive T cell circulates thru a lymph node every 12-24hrs

●​ Macrophages that are present in peripheral tissue dn exit the peripheral tissue, unlike
  DC

●​  In mucosal areas - say a cut in your mouth
       ○​ Pathogens enter and encounter immature DC under epithelial cell layer
       ○​ Immature DC take up pathogen and some will enter lymphatics and enter
           regional lymph node
       ○​ Some pathogens that entered can also enter MALT tissues via the M cell (ie
            tonsils in mouth)
       ○​ Naive T cells will be responding to pathogen’s peptides in both MALT tissues and
         lymph nodes

●​ Fun fact: Of the 800 lymph nodes present in the human body, 300 are in the neck region
       ○​  This is bc most pathogens enter our body via our mouth

●​  Recall: T cell immunity always initiated in SLT by mature dendritic cell to naive T cell

Topic 8-1

    ●​ B cell immunity aka Humoral Immunity





    ●​ B cell immunity results in production of antibodies to help eliminate extracellular
      pathogens and inactive soluble proteins (e.g toxins)

    ●​ Each B cell expresses multiple copies (approx 100,000) of a B cell receptor (BCR) with
        specificity to one unique antigenic epitope
           ○​ BCR is an immunoglobulin (Ig) molecule
                   ■​  structure: 2 identical heavy chains & 2 identical light chains; see right
                  image for constant region and variable regions
                   ■​  Ig molec expresses 2 identical variable regions - can bind to the same
                      antigenic epitope twice
           ○​ Can bind directly to antigenic epitope, unlike T cell receptor
           ○​ TCR can only bind to to one peptide (protein antigen); BCR can bind directly to
             both a protein and non-protein antigens

●​ Reviewing a primary immune response:
       ○​  With B cell immunity, the naive B cell is circulating through the secondary
          lymphoid tissues -> if it finds its unique protein antigenic epitope it will bind
            directly to it (could be on extracellular pathogen or soluble protein)
       ○​ Naive B cell then undergoes clonal expansion
       ○​ Daughter cells will become either effector cells (aka plasma cells) or memory B
            cells
       ○​  Different from T cell immunity where all daughter T cells become effector
           cells
       ○​  Primary B cell response to a protein antigen generates plasma cells and memory
       B cells
